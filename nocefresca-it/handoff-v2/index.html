You’ll love it if you want to…

  • Take a step back. You need some quiet time and space to reflect and recharge.
  • Focus on your practice. You want to develop new work or bring an ongoing project forward.
  • Explore a line of research. You are curious about a subject, a context or a question you want to investigate.
  • Experiment. You are looking for the freedom to test ideas, work with unfamiliar approaches and discover new possibilities.
  • Receive curatorial support. You value exchanging ideas and reflecting on your research and creative process.
  • Be part of a small community. You enjoy sharing ideas, conversations and everyday moments with others.

  • You want a beach holiday or nightlife. Milis is a village of 1,500 people. The sea is 19 km away and you’ll need a car to explore.
  • You need a structured course. The residency is self-directed: you’re expected to be self-sufficient in your practice.
  • You need a fully funded fellowship. Nocefresca can’t offer fellowships at the moment — but can support your own funding applications.

Location - Milis, Sardinia

A citrus village at the foot of Montiferru.

Milis is an ancient village in west-central Sardinia — close to the western coast, yet far from the tourist crowds. In spring, the scent of orange blossoms fills the whole village.

  • 1,500inhabitants — markets, cafés, a bakery, a pharmacy, an artisanal pasta factory and a Wednesday farmers’ market
  • 19 kmto the beaches of Sardinia’s west coast
  • 20 kmto Oristano, the nearest city, with art shops and hardware stores
  • 100 kmto Cagliari, the island’s capital, and its airport
  • 13th c.when Camaldolese monks planted the citrus groves that still surround the town
Getting here

Fly into Cagliari, take a frequent train to Oristano, then a bus (about 30 minutes) or a pre-booked taxi with the residency’s trusted drivers. You can also fly into Olbia or Alghero, but public transport from there is more limited.

Once your place is confirmed you receive a pre-departure guide with detailed travel information.

Getting around

Many artists stayed two or three months without a car and enjoyed it. The residency’s bikes (three gears) are perfect for the village and the countryside. To explore the island, renting a car is strongly advised — residents often share one. Milis–Oristano–Cagliari is easy by public transport; Nuoro, Alghero and the beaches are much harder.

Why Sardinia?

Living in a rural village, close to local communities and far from tourists and chaotic cities, brings moments of authentic simplicity. Expect wide-open landscapes, centuries-old trees, rocks shaped by wind and sea, megalithic architecture and traces of prehistoric societies — on an island that hosts one of the world’s five Blue Zones.

Spaces - Accomodation

A home within the village.

Local residents open their vacant homes to the residency in the quieter months. For a month or three, you become a temporary inhabitant of a Sardinian village.

Located a short walk from the studios, these houses retain traces of the people who have lived in them.

They are homes, not tourist accommodation. Artists are expected to take care of them as temporary residents, with the same responsibility they would have in their own homes.

  • A private bedroom in a shared house (max 4 artists) — or a suite, en-suite room or cottage
  • Fully equipped kitchen, washing machine, Wi-Fi, heating and cooling
  • Bed linen, towels and kitchen basics provided
  • General cleaning at the start and end of your stay
  • Free street parking · you handle your own recycling

How you’ll eat

Cook in your kitchen with produce from the Wednesday farmers’ market, the two butchers and the bakery off the square — or book Sardinian and international meals cooked by Antonella, a local farmer, at Casa Bagnolo. Bigger supermarkets are in Oristano, 20 minutes away. You won’t go hungry.

A glimpse of some homes — availability changes throughout the year.

Accessibility

On the ground floor of Casa Bagnolo, two studios and two desk spaces are physically accessible to wheelchair users and people with limited mobility, as is the courtyard. The upper floor is not accessible at the moment. Each house is different: write to us and we’ll describe them in detail.

Programme

The Monthly Programme: Your Own Path, with Support Along the Way.

The residency is primarily self-directed: you’re expected to be self-sufficient in your practice. Around your work, Nocefresca builds a light structure to feed it — and none of it is mandatory.

  1. 01

    Before you arrive

    • An online pre-departure meeting with your group and the Nocefresca team, to share interests and shape the residency
    • Practical needs sorted in advance: studio, housing, equipment
    • If you need one, an invitation letter and a residency plan with a personalised budget

  2. 02

    During the month

    • Present your practice to the group
    • Two group meetings and three one-to-one sessions with the guest curator
    • Join talks and workshops hosted by fellow residents
    • Take part in activities organised by the residency around the yearly theme
    • Spontaneous extras: excursions, screenings, visiting artists, social life with the village

  3. 03

    At the end

    • A final public open studio event
    • A text by the curator on your practice
    • Your profile in the Nocefresca online archive

The only thing we ask

During your stay, the gift of your creative input, in any form: cooking your favourite recipe at a public dinner, a workshop for local artists, a public talk, a service built on your passions. It isn’t mandatory — but it’s how a lasting community of artists grows here.

A theme each year — a lens, never a brief

You are free to develop your own project, with no restriction on topic or outcome. Each year, a different theme offers a starting point for investigating the territory — its landscapes, histories, cultures, people and ongoing transformations — beyond the familiar narratives of tourism.

How curatorial support works

Each residency group is followed by a guest curator throughout the month: two group meetings and three individual sessions with each artist, with ongoing feedback and critical exchange. The curator also writes texts on the participating artists, documenting and contextualising their practices.

The team is on site or on call for everything else — finding equipment or materials, talking to people who don’t speak English, connecting you with local collaborators, suggesting sites relevant to your research, scouting locations for site-specific work and handling permits.

Applying

Who can apply?

Nocefresca is perfect for — but not limited to — visual artists, writers, designers, composers, film-makers, illustrators, mixed-media artists, architects, fashion designers, researchers and community artists. Solo artists, collectives and small groups of all ages are welcome.

Do I need a specific project? Is a final outcome required?

There are no required outcomes: Nocefresca is a research-based residency focused on experimentation and exchange. Your project idea is still an important part of the application, because it helps us understand your practice, interests and research approach.

Can I apply after the deadline?

Yes, if there are still places available. The main deadline for Open Call 2027 was 7 September 2026. If places remain in the period you want, you can still apply; if not, you can wait for the next call or join a two-week short stay (Nocefreschissima). It is also possible to apply at any time if you already have specific funds to cover your participation.

How does selection work?

The selection aims to create a harmonious group. Criteria are artistic quality, feasibility of the project, length of stay, impact on the group and on the local community, and secured budget.

Do you offer fellowships or funding?

Not at the moment. If you are selected, we provide a formal invitation letter and, if needed, an official residency plan with a personalised budget to support your funding applications. The open call is published a year ahead partly to give artists time to find funding.

The residency

How long can I stay?

One, two or three months, in the periods between March and June and between September and December 2027. In the form you choose up to three preferred starting months; if selected we’ll do our best to meet your request, though periods may be changed with notice to design a balanced group.

What is included in the fee — and what isn’t?

Included: accommodation (utilities, kitchenware, towels and bed linen, washing machine, Wi-Fi, street parking, cleaning), 24/7 studio access, equipment, yoga mats and bikes, curatorial assistance, scheduled activities such as open studios, workshops, talks and trips, and your profile in the online archive. Not included: travel, medical insurance, food, production costs, consumable materials and extra services.

How many artists are there at the same time?

Up to 7 residents, plus up to 2 short-stay guests.

Are the group activities mandatory?

No. Whether you share a flat or live privately, you decide how much time to give to activities. We encourage them as a way to connect with each other and better understand the Sardinian context — the final open studio is optional too.

Practical

Do I need to speak Italian?

Not essential. The staff at Casa Bagnolo and the other residents speak English. Milis is rural and not many people speak English, so some Italian helps — learn to order your cappuccino and vino rosso beforehand! Online lessons can be arranged.

How do I get there, and do I need a car?

Fly into Cagliari, take a train to Oristano, then a bus (about 30 minutes) or a pre-booked taxi with our trusted drivers. Olbia and Alghero airports are possible but connections are more limited. Many artists stayed without a car; to explore the island, renting one (and sharing it) is strongly advised. Bikes are available for the village. You receive a pre-departure guide once confirmed.

Can I bring a guest, children or a pet?

One guest can share your room and bed (€15 per night, minimum one week). Children are welcome only if you request a private apartment, and cannot stay at the studio premises. Pets can’t be accommodated at the moment.

Are the studios and houses accessible?

On the ground floor of Casa Bagnolo, two studios and two desk spaces are accessible to wheelchair users and people with limited mobility, and so is the courtyard; the upper floor is not. Each house is different — write to us and we’ll tell you more.

What are the payment, cancellation and rescheduling terms?

If selected, you sign a confirmation letter and pay a €450 confirmation fee within 2 weeks (non-refundable; it secures your place). The rest is due 12 weeks before you start. Cancel 8+ weeks before: full refund minus the confirmation fee; 8–4 weeks: 50% refund minus the confirmation fee; under 4 weeks: no refund. Rescheduling costs €80 with at least 8 weeks’ notice, within the same calendar year and subject to availability.
